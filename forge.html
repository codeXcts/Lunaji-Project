        Grammar §2.7, ruled 2026-10-11. A compound is one word, written joined, with one accent domain. The head is last and gives the whole word its class and its animacy; the first element says which kind, and may be a noun or a verb — a verb keeps its theme -u. Nothing ends in a verb. At the seam: R12 voices the head's first obstruent unless it already holds a voiced one; a nasal-final first element links into a vowel; a nasal vowel may stand before a nasal consonant; of two identical vowels the first element's drops, with no apostrophe. The head is never touched; the first element gives way: a nasal-final first element links even before its own vowel; two back vowels merge by R11, the head's vowel surviving; u and ü count as the same vowel, so a verb's -u drops before a u- or ü-head; an o drops before a ü-head (Phonology §4.3).

        Compound first when both hold. Coin instead when either fails — the parts do not add up, as a butterfly is not a kind of fly — or when the meaning is core tier: said daily, drawn at two or three syllables. A compound is almost always four syllables or more, and the most frequent words stay short. “Bed” stays õkiwe, though “sleep-thing” could be built.

        Transitivity — does the verb take an object?

        2026-10-29. Each verb gets tr: t takes an object (ni), i takes none, b both (open the door / the door opens). The Composer reads it, and so does the causative: under tsobã or idovu the one caused to act takes ni with an intransitive verb and di with a transitive one (Grammar §4.10).         Proto R15, final vowel loss in nouns — ruled 2026-10-29. A noun's final oral vowel is lost after a single r, l, s, z or sh that follows an oral vowel: hotori → hotor, posha → posh. It runs after R14. Not where only a vowel and a consonant would be left (ole, elo, ora stay); not where a native ü would lose the i after it (tsüri stays); never on emose; a loan only where its source word ends in that consonant. Verbs, nasal-final nouns and numerals are untouched. It is a regular rule: Derive and Draw run it on every noun from this build on, and Borrow keeps such a final consonant on a noun.
        
  
        The topic after a final consonant is -a, and the consonant links into it: gitar + a → gitara. Every other particle and ending meets the consonant: gitarni, masta, hotorjo. Inside a phrase the consonant links into a following vowel-initial word (gitar uã). A closed final syllable is heavy.
        
  
        
    
    
        
  
        
  
        
    
        Proto R16, the irregular verbs — ruled 2026-10-29. Eight verbs, where an ending meets the stem directly: hi · hize · hinena · hinenaze (was hĩ); yu · yuze · yuena · yuenaze, yozuri; kazõ · kazõse · kazena · kazenase; shĩyu · shĩze · shinena · shinenaze; onu · õze · onena · onenaze; shimu · shimuze · shimena · shimenaze; sofu · sofuze · sofena · sofenaze; kuwanu · kuwãze · kuwanena · kuwanenaze. Every form, Paradigm and the phrase tools use these from this build. Apply renames hĩ to hi, gives the eight entries an irr field, and respells stored lines.
