    Every consonant with every vowel. Each letter opens with the moon curl. The chart follows the Brush or Carved setting above.

    These lists decide how words are written in Blocks and rows. One entry per line. They are saved in this browser, so export the Forge lexicon habitually and keep these in step with it.

    Two ways of writing

    • Blocks and rows is the fluent form. A native stem is packed into one square block. Particles, endings and function words follow in small glyphs, and loanwords are written in a row, the way Japanese writes foreign words in katakana. A compound is two blocks touching.
    • Row only is the learner's form. Every glyph is full size, one sound after another, spelled exactly like the Latin spelling. Both are correct Lunaji.

    Typing

    • Nasal vowels: type ã ĩ õ, or a~ i~ o~.
    • - splits a stem from its endings yourself: lõji-wa. Use it when the app guesses the stem wrong, or to stop a split.
    • + joins compound blocks: koime+zaĩko.
    • * in front of a word writes it as a loan: *jura.

    How the app finds a stem

    It takes common particles and endings (ba, ni, pe, wa, di, na, -ze, -ri, -ta, -jo) off the end of a word, but only while at least two syllables remain and the word is not on the whole-stems list. Stand-alone function words like hena and mi are always written in the small glyphs.

    The letters

    • Consonant families follow the mouth: the lips share an open frame, the tongue tip a central vertical, the palate a cliff stroke, the back of the mouth a Z-shaped turn. A nasal consonant adds one horizontal. Voicing is the two ticks Japanese uses.
    • Vowels: none for u, a sweep on the right for a, a vertical on the left for i, two short bars for e, a left sweep for o. A nasal vowel adds a long sweep along the foot.
    • Glides and clusters are a stroke over the letter: a dot for y, two dots for w, a short sweep for r, a short bar for l.
    • A coda ends with a heavy stop dot. A long consonant is written as a coda of the same consonant followed by the syllable, like Japanese small tsu.
